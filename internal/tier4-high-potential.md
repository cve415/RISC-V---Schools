## Tier 4: High-Potential Programs (No Clear RISC-V Coursework Yet)

*These institutions have established, reputable computer engineering and/or electrical engineering programs, but there is currently no strong public evidence of sustained RISC-V coursework or labs. They are prime candidates for future RISC-V curriculum development and partnership outreach.*

- **California Institute of Technology (Caltech)**  
  Small, highly selective program with deep strength in digital systems, embedded hardware, and VLSI design [web:83][web:82]. Ideal for advanced RISC-V microarchitecture and accelerator design modules.

- **Harvard University**  
  Strong SEAS offerings in computer architecture, systems, and hardware security [web:83]. RISC-V could fit into advanced architecture or secure hardware tracks.

- **North Carolina State University**  
  Frequently cited as a solid ECE program with good computer engineering depth, especially for students not targeting the very top-5 schools [web:81]. Good match for RISC-V-based undergraduate architecture labs.

- **Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI)**  
  Longstanding reputation in ECE and computer systems; suitable for RISC-V-focused digital design, FPGA, and SoC coursework [web:82].

- **Virginia Tech** *(core ECE beyond current scattered RISC-V mentions)*  
  Large and influential ECE program; formalizing a RISC-V lab sequence would quickly reach many students [web:82][web:83].

- **Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI)**  
  Known for project-based engineering education [web:83]. RISC-V aligns naturally with capstone design and embedded systems projects.

- **Florida State University**  
  Features in engineering rankings with growing computing and ECE programs [web:83]. RISC-V could differentiate their architecture and embedded courses.

- **Florida Institute of Technology**  
  Private STEM-focused university with solid ECE and CE programs [web:83]. Strong candidate for integrated RISC-V + FPGA teaching labs.

- **New Mexico State University (Main Campus)**  
  Recognized engineering offerings with room to expand open-ISA content [web:83]. Good fit for affordable, open-hardware oriented curriculum.

- **Oakland University**  
  Regional engineering school with ECE/CE programs; RISC-V could be introduced through existing digital logic and computer architecture sequences [web:83].

- **Baylor University**  
  Engineering programs include ECE and CS; RISC-V adoption would support modern architecture and embedded courses [web:83].

- **Clarkson University**  
  Known for hands-on engineering and co-op culture [web:83]. RISC-V would strengthen systems and embedded teaching.

- **Wayne State University**  
  Offers ECE and CS programs with industry-connected projects [web:83]. A RISC-V teaching flow would be attractive for local semiconductor/embedded employers.
