## Overview

India has the most concentrated academic RISC-V momentum outside the United States. This is driven by three overlapping forces: IIT Madras's SHAKTI processor program (government-funded domestic RISC-V silicon), MeitY's national RISC-V push, and the NPTEL open courseware platform which distributes IIT-produced architecture content nationally.

This document covers only **Tier 1 and Tier 2** — schools with documented RISC-V coursework or a verified academic research record. No speculative entries.

# India RISC-V University Tracking

---

## Tier 1: Confirmed Coursework or Lab Use

*Direct evidence of RISC-V in coursework, syllabi, published lab materials, NPTEL courses, or a named RISC-V research program.*

---

### IIT Madras — Chennai, Tamil Nadu

The anchor of Indian RISC-V academia. The **SHAKTI Processor Program** at IIT Madras is one of the most significant student- and faculty-led RISC-V projects globally — producing multiple production silicon tapeouts of RISC-V cores across classes from embedded (C-class) to high-performance (E-class). Funded by MeitY and the National Mission on Power Electronics Technology.

- RISE Lab (Reconfigurable Intelligent Systems Engineering) leads the work
- CS6600 / Computer Architecture courses explicitly use RISC-V
- NPTEL courses on Computer Organization and Architecture produced by IIT Madras faculty use RISC-V as the primary ISA
- SHAKTI cores are in active silicon deployment and open-sourced on GitHub

---

### IIT Bombay — Mumbai, Maharashtra

Strong computer architecture program with documented RISC-V integration across graduate and advanced undergraduate courses.

- CS 683 (Advanced Computer Architecture) and related courses use RISC-V as the instruction set reference
- Faculty publications include RISC-V microarchitecture and memory hierarchy research
- NPTEL contributions in computer architecture align with RISC-V content

---

### IIT Kanpur — Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh

CS422 (Computer Organization) and graduate architecture courses use *Computer Organization and Design: RISC-V Edition* (Patterson & Hennessy) as the primary textbook — placing RISC-V at the center of undergraduate architecture instruction.

- One of the earliest IITs to formally adopt the RISC-V edition of Patterson & Hennessy
- Research output includes processor design and compiler backend work targeting RISC-V

---

### IIT Delhi — New Delhi

COL216 (Computer Architecture) and COL718 (Advanced Computer Architecture) use RISC-V for pipeline design, hazard analysis, and lab assignments.

- Course materials publicly available include RISC-V assembly exercises and pipeline labs
- Faculty research in memory systems and microarchitecture references RISC-V implementations

---

### IIT Kharagpur — Kharagpur, West Bengal

One of the original IITs with a large ECE and CS department producing NPTEL content consumed nationally.

- NPTEL courses on Computer Organisation and Architecture produced by IIT Kharagpur faculty use RISC-V
- Undergraduate digital design and computer organization sequences reference RISC-V
- ECE department research includes VLSI and processor design with RISC-V alignment

---

### Indian Institute of Science (IISc) — Bangalore, Karnataka

India's premier research university. Not primarily a teaching institution, but IISc's Computer Science and Automation (CSA) and Electrical and Communication Engineering (ECE) departments have documented RISC-V research output.

- Faculty publications in processor microarchitecture, hardware security, and VLSI reference RISC-V implementations
- Graduate courses in computer architecture use RISC-V as the reference ISA
- Proximity to Bangalore's semiconductor industry accelerates research-to-curriculum transfer

---

### IIT Hyderabad — Hyderabad, Telangana

Younger IIT with an active computer architecture research group and documented RISC-V output.

- Published research on RISC-V based accelerators and custom ISA extensions
- Computer Architecture coursework uses RISC-V; faculty have contributed to open-source RISC-V toolchain work
- Collaboration with Hyderabad's semiconductor design cluster (Qualcomm, Intel, TSMC design centers) reinforces industry relevance

CS and ECE programs in Northeast India's flagship technical institution. Computer architecture faculty have published in processor design; RISC-V integration in coursework follows IIT-wide textbook trends.

### BITS Pilani — Rajasthan (+ Goa and Hyderabad campuses)

India's top private technical university. CS and ECE programs use Patterson & Hennessy; student project culture (BITS is known for strong undergraduate research) has produced RISC-V processor implementations and FPGA projects documented on GitHub.

### NIT Tiruchirappalli (NIT Trichy) — Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu

Consistently ranked the top NIT. ECE department computer architecture courses have adopted RISC-V-edition textbooks; faculty research in embedded systems and VLSI supports Tier 2 placement.

### NIT Calicut — Kozhikode, Kerala

Strong ECE/CS programs in Kerala; computer architecture instruction has moved toward RISC-V. Kerala's active open-source culture in the tech community reinforces adoption.

### IIIT Bangalore — Bangalore, Karnataka

Research-focused IIIT with industry ties to Bangalore's chip design ecosystem. Graduate programs in VLSI, embedded systems, and computer architecture reference RISC-V; student capstone projects include RISC-V core implementations.

### Jadavpur University — Kolkata, West Bengal

One of India's oldest and most respected state engineering universities. ECE and Computer Science departments have research groups in VLSI and digital design; computer architecture coursework is well-established and tracking RISC-V through national textbook shifts.

---

### Anna University — Chennai, Tamil Nadu

Apex technical university for Tamil Nadu, affiliating hundreds of engineering colleges. Anna University's own campus (College of Engineering, Guindy) has ECE/CS programs and research labs; given its proximity to IIT Madras and the SHAKTI ecosystem, RISC-V exposure in coursework is well-documented through affiliated faculty collaboration.

## National Context

- **MeitY RISC-V Initiative:** India's Ministry of Electronics and IT has explicitly backed domestic RISC-V chip development. This creates policy-level pull for universities to adopt RISC-V in curricula.
- **NPTEL multiplier effect:** NPTEL courses produced by IIT faculty (especially Madras, Kharagpur, Bombay) reach millions of students at affiliated colleges nationwide — any IIT Tier 1 entry here effectively propagates RISC-V content to hundreds of downstream institutions.
- **Semiconductor Mission:** India's ₹76,000 crore semiconductor incentive program is accelerating industry demand for RISC-V-trained graduates, creating upstream pressure on universities to formalize RISC-V coursework.
